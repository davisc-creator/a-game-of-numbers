    How it works

    Pick an era and a stat and a leaderboard gets locked in behind the scenes. Nobody sees it.

    Drafters take turns naming players in snake order: 1‑2‑3‑4, then 4‑3‑2‑1, back and forth.

    Name someone on the list and you score their rank. The 137th‑best player is worth 137. Deep cuts pay more than obvious ones — that's the whole game.

    Once a player is named, he's off the board for everyone. Tied players share a rank and are worth the same.

    Foul ball. Name someone in the ten spots just past the end of the list and you've fouled it off. It costs a strike if you have fewer than two; with two strikes it's free. Either way your turn ends. Each of those ten only saves you once.

    Anyone else is a strike — but you'll still be told exactly what he did and where he ranked. Three strikes and you're done drafting; everyone else keeps going.

    Typos and last names are forgiven. Naming someone already taken costs nothing.

    Most points when the dust settles wins.

    Who's drafting

    Up to four, pass-and-play. With more than one, the spin is shared and picks go in snake order — a player taken is gone for everyone.

    How it works

    Spin a club and a ten-year window. Everyone who played for them in that stretch comes up as a card. Take one, and keep going until you have a full roster: nine in the field, four on the bench, five starters, two in relief and a closer. Three respins of the club, three of the era.

    Every card shows OPS+ and ERA−, measured against the leagues that player actually played in — a 1968 arm and a 1999 arm arrive comparable. Then the roster plays 162 games and you find out how it did.
