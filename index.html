    How it works

    Pick an era and a stat and a leaderboard gets locked in behind the scenes. Nobody sees it.

    Drafters take turns naming players in snake order: 1‑2‑3‑4, then 4‑3‑2‑1, back and forth.

    Only the top 100 score. Name one of them and you get his rank — the 87th‑best player is worth 87. Deep cuts pay more than obvious ones; that's the whole game.

    Once a player is named, he's off the board for everyone. Tied players share a rank and are worth the same, so if sixty men tie for 50th they are all worth 50.

    101 to 125 is a foul ball. It costs a strike if you have fewer than two; with two strikes it's free. Either way your turn ends, and each of those twenty-five only saves you once.

    Name the number one player and your foul band stretches to 140 — for one pick, and only when you're on two strikes. Rank 1 is worth one point, the least on the board, so it pays in rope instead: with two strikes against you, a pick that lands between 126 and 140 is a free foul instead of strike three. At no strikes or one it's an ordinary strike and the extension is kept for when it matters. If two men tie for first, either one earns it, and the seat panel shows it until it's spent.

    126 and beyond is a strike (141 and beyond with the extension), however good he was. You'll still be told exactly what he did and where he ranked — the lists run hundreds deep, so you can see how close you came. Three strikes and you're done drafting; everyone else keeps going.

    Stuck for an era? Surprise me on the setup screen rolls a random era and stat — including spans nobody has a board for.

    Typos and last names are forgiven. Naming someone already taken costs nothing, and if the name you typed isn't an exact match you'll be offered a short list of who you might have meant — drawn from the whole era, not the leaderboard, so the list gives nothing away.

    Most points when the dust settles wins.

    Other ways to play

    Solo practice. One drafter, the same board and the same rules, nobody to beat. Pick "Until you are out" and see how far you get before three strikes. Solo games are kept in your records but never count as a win; the results screen tells you your best previous score on that exact board.

    Series. Also under Format, with two or more drafters. A run of ordinary games under one tally — best of N, first to N wins, first to N points, or a fixed number — with the era, the category or both re-rolled between games if you like. A drawn game advances nobody. The opening pick rotates from game to game.

    World Series. Under Format on the setup screen, once there are two or more drafters. Best of seven, and every game is a random era and a random stat. Choose 1, 3, 5 or 7 players each per game.

    The picks stay hidden until everyone has named somebody — then they're turned over together and the most points takes the game. Nobody sees a rank, a name or a score until then, so the last to pick isn't handed the number to beat.

    Four wins takes the title. With three or more drafters, two tying at the top have still beaten everyone else, so both get the win; only the whole table being level is a draw, and a draw advances nobody. Level on wins after seven and total points decides it; level on both and it goes to sudden death. Eras are drawn evenly across single seasons, decades, long spans and spans nobody has a board for — 1962 to 1968, say, built on the spot — so it isn't 1943 seven times.

    Records are kept on this device only and are never sent anywhere. Export and Import on the Records screen move them between phones.

    Who's drafting

    Up to four, pass-and-play. With more than one, the spin is shared and picks go in snake order — a player taken is gone for everyone.

    How it works

    Spin a club and a ten-year window. Everyone who played for them in that stretch comes up as a card. Take one, and keep going until you have a full roster: nine hitters — the eight positions and a DH — then three starters, two in relief and a closer. Fifteen in all. Three respins of the club, three of the era.

    There is no bench, so every hitter has to fit a real position. If a club has nobody you can use, the respin is free.

    Every card shows OPS+ and ERA−, measured against the leagues that player actually played in — a 1968 arm and a 1999 arm arrive comparable. Then the roster plays 162 games and you find out how it did.
